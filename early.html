Early episodes · free · no sign-up

Watch the whole rigging unit before it's on YouTube

New CrewForge episodes are finished weeks ahead of their YouTube date. The whole rigging unit, 12 episodes, goes public on YouTube together on Mon Nov 30. Watch every one here now, free and with no ads.

ON YOUTUBE MON NOV 30

Mechanical Advantage: Block and Tackle and Chain Falls for the Millwright Exam

How much do you have to pull to lift a load with a block and tackle or a chain fall? In this lesson we use a live simulator to count parts of line, see why the lead line counts only when it leaves the moving block, measure how far the lead line travels, and add sheave friction with the 10 % rule and a maker's table.

Early copy, no ads · 15 min · AI narration

ON YOUTUBE MON NOV 30

Load Weight and Centre of Gravity: Rigging Math for the Millwright Exam

Before any lift you need two numbers: how much it weighs and where its centre of gravity is. In this lesson we use a live simulator to weigh steel plate, round bar and pipe from their dimensions (490 lb/ft³, 7,850 kg/m³), find the centre of gravity of a skid with a motor on it, work out what each leg carries when the picks are uneven, and see what happens when the centre of gravity sits outside the picks or the hook is off the CG.

Early copy, no ads · 14 min · AI narration · The free lesson for this episode

ON YOUTUBE MON NOV 30

Rigging Hardware: Eyebolts, Shackles, WLL and D/d Ratio for the Millwright Exam

The working load limit stamped on rigging hardware only holds for a straight pull. In this lesson we use a live simulator to see what an angled pull does to a shoulder eyebolt, why plain eyebolts are for in-line pulls only, how side loading cuts a shackle's rating, and why wire rope bent around a small pin loses strength (the D/d ratio).

Early copy, no ads · 12 min · AI narration

ON YOUTUBE MON NOV 30

Hitches, Knots and Load Control: Choker, Basket and Tag Lines for the Millwright Exam

One sling, four hitches, four different capacities. In this lesson we use a live simulator to see what a choker does to a sling's rating, how the choke angle cuts it further, why a basket can double the rating or lose a big share of it (sling angle and the wire rope D/d ratio), which knots riggers use and why, and why a load needs tag lines until it lands.

Early copy, no ads · 12 min · AI narration · The free lesson for this episode

ON YOUTUBE MON NOV 30

Reading Crane Load Charts: Gross Load, Radius and Deflection for the Millwright Exam

The chart says 20,000 lb and the load weighs 19,500 lb. Is there 500 lb to spare?

Early copy, no ads · 12 min · AI narration

ON YOUTUBE MON NOV 30

Crane Hand Signals and Lift Communication for the Millwright Exam

Who can stop the crane? In this lesson a live simulator shows every standard mobile crane hand signal with the crane answering it: hoist, lower, main hoist, whip line, raise and lower the boom, the two combined boom-and-load signals, swing, extend and retract, move slowly, stop, emergency stop and dog everything.

Early copy, no ads · 12 min · AI narration · The free lesson for this episode

ON YOUTUBE MON NOV 30

Wire Rope Inspection: Broken Wires, Diameter and Discard Criteria for the Millwright Exam

Would you lift with this rope? In this lesson we use a live simulator to read a wire rope's construction (6 × 19 and 6 × 36, IWRC and fibre core, regular and Lang lay), count broken wires in one rope lay, measure diameter across the crowns, and spot the damage that takes a sling out of service: kinks, birdcaging, crushing, heat and corrosion.

Early copy, no ads · 12 min · AI narration

ON YOUTUBE MON NOV 30

Synthetic Slings: Tags, Capacity, Edge Protection and Inspection for the Millwright Exam

Why did a sling rated for almost 11,000 lb drop a 6,000 lb load? In this lesson we use a live simulator to read a synthetic sling tag, work out capacity in vertical, choker and basket hitches, protect sharp edges, and spot the damage that takes web slings and roundslings out of service: cuts, red core yarns, burns, broken stitching, knots, sun damage, chemicals and heat.

Early copy, no ads · 12 min · AI narration

ON YOUTUBE MON NOV 30

Jacks, Rollers and Skidding Loads: Load Share, F = P × A, Cribbing and Slopes for the Millwright Exam

A jack rated for the load can still drop it. In this lesson we use a live simulator to work out how much one jack carries when one end of a machine stays down, read a hydraulic jack with F = P × A, crib as you go, and compare the pull needed to skid, roll or skate a load: dry steel, greased steel, rollers and skates, on the level and on a slope, with the control line that stops a runaway.

Early copy, no ads · 13 min · AI narration

ON YOUTUBE MON NOV 30

Lift Planning and Critical Lifts: Percent of Chart, Tandem Lifts, Wind and Power Lines

Ninety-two percent of the chart, two cranes on one beam, a 28 mph wind: which makes it a critical lift? In this lesson a live crane simulator builds a lift plan: gross load and percent of chart at the loaded radius, the critical-lift triggers in British Columbia's regulation (over 90 % beyond half the radius, tandem lifts over 75 %, work platforms, energized lines) and Alberta's 75 % lift calculation, tandem load shares from the centre of gravity, wind limits, Ontario's power-line limits of approach and what goes in the written plan.

Early copy, no ads · 12 min · AI narration

ON YOUTUBE MON NOV 30

Tugger Winches and Fleet Angle: Lead Sheave Distance, Line Pull by Layer, Snatch Block Anchors for the Millwright Exam

A tugger pulling a tenth of its rating can still throw its rope off the drum. In this lesson we use a live simulator to work out fleet angle and lead sheave distance for smooth and grooved drums, see why line pull drops as the drum fills, keep enough wraps on the drum, and find the anchor load on a snatch block from the angle between the lines, with the bight you never stand in.

Early copy, no ads · 13 min · AI narration

ON YOUTUBE MON NOV 30

Wire Rope Terminations: Clips, Wedge Sockets, Swaged Fittings and Efficiency for the Millwright Exam

Two clips on a half inch rope, and the eye pulls out under a 4,000 lb load. In this lesson we use a live simulator to install wire rope clips the maker's way (number of clips, turn-back, torque, saddle on the live end, re-torque after the first load), set up a wedge socket (live line in line with the pin, tail length), and compare termination efficiencies: clips, wedge sockets, pressed sleeves, hand splices, swaged and spelter sockets.

Early copy, no ads · 12 min · AI narration
