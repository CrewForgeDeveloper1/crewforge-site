B. Rigging, hoisting/lifting and moving 17 of 135 exam questions (13 %)

QUESTION 1 OF 30

A 12,000 lb load is lifted with a two-leg bridle at 60° from horizontal. What is the tension in each leg?

Show the answer

Answer: B. 6,930 lb

(12,000 ÷ 2) × 1.155 = 6,930 lb

Half the load is 6,000 lb. 60° gives a factor of 1.155. 6,000 times 1.155 is 6,930 lb.

If you picked A, you forgot the angle factor.

QUESTION 2 OF 30

A 3,000 kg load hangs from a two-leg bridle with a 120° included angle between the legs. What is the tension in each leg?

Show the answer

Answer: C. 3,000 kg

120° included → 30° from horizontal → (3,000 ÷ 2) × 2.000 = 3,000 kg

Half of 120 is 60. 90 minus 60 is 30° from horizontal, and the factor is 2. 1,500 kg times 2 is 3,000 kg in each leg.

If you used 60° as the sling angle, you got B, and you'd have rigged a lift that's seriously overloaded.

QUESTION 3 OF 30

A 6,000 lb load hangs from a tackle with 4 parts of line holding the moving block. Ignoring friction, what pull is needed on the lead line?

Show the answer

Answer: B. 1,500 lb

P = W ÷ n = 6,000 ÷ 4 = 1,500 lb

The mechanical advantage is the number of parts holding the moving block, four. 6,000 divided by 4 is 1,500.

If you got C, you divided by 3. Count every part at the moving block, including the dead end.

Learn the topic: Block and tackle mechanical advantage

QUESTION 4 OF 30

A tackle has a two-sheave upper block and a one-sheave lower block. The lead line leaves the upper block. What is the ideal mechanical advantage?

Show the answer

Answer: B. 3

Dead end on the lower block + 2 parts = 3 parts hold the moving block; lead line not counted

Picture the reeving. The rope is dead ended on the lower block. It runs up over the first upper sheave, down around the lower sheave, up over the second upper sheave, and down to your hands. Three parts hold the lower block. The lead line leaves the fixed block, so it doesn't count.

If you said four, you counted the lead line.

QUESTION 5 OF 30

A steel plate is 10 ft × 4 ft × 1 in thick. Using 490 lb/ft³ for steel, what does it weigh?

Show the answer

Answer: B. 1,633 lb

V = 10 × 4 × 1/12 = 3.333 ft³; W = 3.333 × 490 = 1,633 lb

Turn the inch into feet first. 1/12 of a foot. 10 times 4 times 1/12 is 3.33 ft³. Times 490 is about 1,633 lb. A shortcut you can check it with: 1 in plate weighs about 40.8 lb/ft². 40 ft² times 40.8 gives the same answer.

If you got D, you left the inch as a foot. That plate would be 12 times too heavy.

QUESTION 6 OF 30

A 12 ft skid weighs 1,960 lb with its CG at the middle. A 3,920 lb motor sits with its CG 9 ft from the left end. Where is the combined CG, measured from the left end?

Show the answer

Answer: C. 8.00 ft

x̄ = (1,960 × 6 + 3,920 × 9) ÷ 5,880 = 47,040 ÷ 5,880 = 8.00 ft

Skid, 1,960 times 6. Motor, 3,920 times 9. Add them. 47,040. Divide by the total, 5,880. 8 ft from the left end.

B is halfway between 6 and 9, which ignores the weights. The motor is twice as heavy, so the centre of gravity sits twice as close to it.

QUESTION 7 OF 30

A shoulder eyebolt rated 13,300 lb in-line is loaded at 15° from in-line. Using the maker's table (15° = 80 %), what is its adjusted WLL?

Show the answer

Answer: C. 10,640 lb

Adjusted WLL = 13,300 × 0.80 = 10,640 lb

Rated limit times the percentage. 13,300 times 0.8. 10,640 lb.

B is the 30° row, and A is the 45° row. Read the row for your angle.

QUESTION 8 OF 30

A plain (no shoulder) eyebolt rated 5,200 lb in-line will be pulled 10° off in-line. What may it carry?

Show the answer

Answer: D. Nothing: in-line pulls only

Plain eyebolts have no angle rating. Any angled pull is not allowed.

A plain eyebolt has no shoulder to take the sideways part of the pull. The maker gives it no angle rating at all.

Straight pulls only. B and C are shoulder eyebolt numbers.

QUESTION 9 OF 30

A wire rope sling is rated 4,000 lb in a vertical hitch. What is its rating in a choker hitch (choke angle 135°)?

Show the answer

Answer: B. 3,000 lb

Choker = 4,000 × 0.75 = 3,000 lb (135° ≥ 120°: no further reduction)

A wire rope choker is 75 % of vertical. 135° is over 120, so there's no more reduction. 4,000 times 0.75. 3,000 lb.

D is a basket with vertical legs. A choker is always less than vertical.

QUESTION 10 OF 30

A wire rope sling is rated 6,000 lb vertical. It is used in a choker with a 75° choke angle. Choker = 75 %; 60–89° choke = 74 %. What is its rating?

Show the answer

Answer: A. 3,330 lb

6,000 × 0.75 × 0.74 = 3,330 lb

Both factors apply. 6,000 times 0.75 is 4,500. Times 0.74 is 3,330.

C stopped after the first factor. B used only the second.

QUESTION 11 OF 30

On a mobile crane load chart, the load radius is measured:

Show the answer

Answer: C. Level, from the centre of rotation to the centre of the hook, with the load on

Radius = level distance from the axis of rotation to the hoist line, with the load hanging

Radius is a level distance. It starts at the centre of rotation, the point the upper works turn on, and it ends at the centre of the hook. And it's measured with the load hanging.

A is the boom length, a different number on the chart. B is near the tipping line, which we'll get to.

QUESTION 12 OF 30

A 12,500 lb load. Hook block 623 lb, slings and shackles 180 lb, a 369 lb ball on the whipline and a 105 lb auxiliary nose. No extension. What gross load do you check against the chart?

Show the answer

Answer: C. 13,777 lb

12,500 + 623 + 180 + 369 + 105 = 13,777 lb

Add everything that hangs from the boom. 12,500, plus 623, plus 180, plus 369, plus 105. 13,777 lb.

A is the load alone, the most common mistake. B forgets the ball and its nose. D adds an extension that isn't there.

QUESTION 13 OF 30

The signaller's arm is extended down, index finger pointing down, and the hand moves in small horizontal circles. What is the signal?

Show the answer

Answer: B. Lower

Index finger down + small circles = LOWER (the load line). Boom signals use the thumb.

A finger pointing down with small circles means lower. It moves the hoist line, so the load comes down.

A is the trap. Lower boom uses the thumb, not the finger, and it does something very different. Here's why that matters.

QUESTION 14 OF 30

Arm extended, fingers closed, thumb pointing down. What is the signal?

Show the answer

Answer: B. Lower boom

Arm out, fist, thumb down = LOWER BOOM (no finger movement)

The thumb means the boom. Thumb down, lower the boom. If all four fingers were opening and closing as well, it would be lower the boom and raise the load.

A uses the index finger. C needs the fingers working.

QUESTION 15 OF 30

A wire rope is marked 6 × 19 IWRC. What does that tell you?

Show the answer

Answer: B. 6 strands, about 19 wires per strand, independent wire rope core

First number = strands · second = wires per strand (class 16–26) · IWRC = independent wire rope core (steel)

The first number is the strands. The second is the wires in each strand, as a class. The letters are the core: independent wire rope core.

A has the numbers backwards. C and D are made up, but they sound right if you're guessing.

QUESTION 16 OF 30

A wire rope sling has 8 broken wires in its worst rope lay. 5 of them are in one strand. What do you do?

Show the answer

Answer: C. Remove it: 5 broken wires in one strand in one lay

Sling limits: 10 in one lay OR 5 in one strand in one lay. Either one removes it.

There are two limits, and either one takes the sling out. 10 in one lay, or five in one strand in one lay. 8 is under 10, but five in one strand is at the limit.

A is the most common mistake: checking the total and forgetting the strand.

QUESTION 17 OF 30

A 1-ton hand chain hoist has an overhaul ratio of 43. How many feet of hand chain do you pull to lift a load 3 ft?

Show the answer

Answer: B. 129 ft

Overhaul ratio = ft of hand chain per ft of lift: 3 × 43 = 129 ft

The overhaul ratio is feet of hand chain for each foot of lift. 3 times 43 is 129 ft.

A is only 1 ft of lift. C divided instead of multiplied. And D used the 58 lb pull, which is a force, not a length.

QUESTION 18 OF 30

A web sling's tag reads: vertical 6,200 lb, choker 4,960 lb, basket 12,400 lb. You'll use it in a choker hitch with the choke angle over 120°. What is the most it can lift?

Show the answer

Answer: B. 4,960 lb

Use the tag's figure for the hitch you're using: choker = 4,960 lb (80 % of vertical, choke ≥ 120°)

Use the figure for the hitch you're actually using. In a choker, that's 4,960 lb.

A is the vertical figure. D doubled the choker, as if it were a basket. It isn't.

QUESTION 19 OF 30

A pump base weighs 9,000 lb and is 8 ft long. Its centre of gravity is 3 ft from the end you're jacking; the other end stays down. What does the jack carry?

Show the answer

Answer: C. 5,625 lb

Moments about the far end: 9,000 × (8 − 3) ÷ 8 = 9,000 × 5 ÷ 8 = 5,625 lb

Take moments about the far end. The weight is 5 ft from it, the jack is 8. 9,000 times 5, divided by 8.

5,625 lb. It's C. A uses the wrong arm, 3 ft instead of 5. B is half the weight. D is the whole weight.

QUESTION 20 OF 30

A lift plan shows a gross load of 9,450 lb and a chart capacity of 13,500 lb at the loaded radius. What percentage of the chart is the lift?

Show the answer

Answer: B. 70.0 %

% of chart = gross ÷ chart capacity = 9,450 ÷ 13,500 = 0.70 → 70.0 %

Gross load divided by the chart capacity. 9,450 divided by 13,500. 0.7. 70 %.

D divided the wrong way round. A used the wrong row of the chart.

QUESTION 21 OF 30

A smooth-drum tugger's drum is 24 in wide between flanges. The lead sheave is 30 ft away, on the drum's centre line. What is the fleet angle, and is it acceptable?

Show the answer

Answer: B. 1.91°, too big for a smooth drum

tan α = 12 in ÷ 360 in = 0.0333 → 1.91°: over the 1.5° smooth-drum limit (it would pass on a grooved drum, 2°)

Half the drum width is 12 in. 30 ft is 360 in. 12 over 360, and the angle is 1.91°.

That's over 1.5, so it's B: too big for a smooth drum. It would pass on a grooved drum. A uses the full width the wrong way. D uses the full width.

QUESTION 22 OF 30

Where must the rated capacity of an overhead crane be marked?

Show the answer

Answer: B. On each side of the crane, readable from the floor, and on the hoist or block

Marked on the crane where the operator can read it, and on the hoist and load block (WorkSafeBC 14.5(1); O. Reg. 851 s.51(1)(c); Alberta OHS Code s.62)

The rated load is marked on the crane where the operator can read it from the floor, usually on both sides of the bridge. The hoist and its load block show their rating too. BC's regulation names all three: the crane, the hoist and the block. That way nobody has to guess what a hoist can lift.

The logbook records inspections, but it doesn't replace the marking. And the pendant marks the controls: up, down and the directions of travel.

QUESTION 23 OF 30

A gantry has a 12 ft span. A 3,000 lb load hangs 4 ft from leg A. Ignoring the gantry's own weight, what does leg A carry?

Show the answer

Answer: C. 2,000 lb

R_A = W × b ÷ L = 3,000 × 8 ÷ 12 = 2,000 lb (leg B: 1,000 lb)

Use the distance to the other leg. 12 minus 4 is 8 ft. 3,000 times 8, over 12, is 2,000 lb. Leg B gets the other 1,000.

A is leg B's share. That's the mistake of using the near distance instead of the far one. The closer the load is to a leg, the more that leg carries.

QUESTION 24 OF 30

Using the Crosby clip table, a 3/4 in wire rope clip eye needs how many clips, how much turn-back and what torque?

Show the answer

Answer: B. 4 clips, 18 in, 130 ft·lb

Crosby G-450 table, 3/4 in: 4 clips minimum, 18 in turn-back, 130 ft·lb

Find the ¾ inch row. Four clips, 18 in turned back, 130 ft·lb.

A is the five eighths row, C is 7 eighths, and D is 1 in. On the exam, read across one row and don't drift.

QUESTION 25 OF 30

A sling eye rides up near the tip of a hoist hook. Why does that cut what the hook can safely carry?

Show the answer

Answer: B. The load acts on a lever arm off the line of the shank and bends the hook open

In the bowl the load is on the line of the shank (no lever arm). Toward the tip, load × lever arm bends the hook open. Makers: never tip load.

In the bowl, the load hangs on the line of the shank. No lever arm. Out toward the tip, the load times the lever arm bends the hook open.

A is wrong because the latch should never carry load. C and D don't change when the eye moves.

QUESTION 26 OF 30

A 3/4 in manila rope has a minimum breaking strength of 4,860 lb. Using a design factor of 5, what is its working load limit?

Show the answer

Answer: C. 972 lb

WLL = breaking strength ÷ design factor = 4,860 ÷ 5 = 972 lb

The working load limit is the breaking strength divided by the design factor. 4,860 over 5.

972 lb. It's C. A is the breaking strength, and you never work there. B divided by 4. D used 10, the factor for holding a person.

QUESTION 27 OF 30

A two-ply web sling about 3/8 in thick goes over a plate edge ground to a 1/4 in radius. Using the rule r ≥ t, is the edge sharp?

Show the answer

Answer: B. Yes: r ÷ t = 0.67, less than 1

r ÷ t = 0.25 ÷ 0.375 = 0.67 < 1 → sharp. Protect the edge or the sling.

Divide the radius by the thickness. A quarter over 3 eighths is about 0.67. Less than one.

It's sharp. B. A is the trap: a rounded corner can still be sharp for a thick sling. C uses one maker's figure for a one ply sling. D waits for damage you can see.

QUESTION 28 OF 30

A 16,000 lb load hangs from a two-leg bridle with no spreader. The slings are at 60° from horizontal. What is the inward squeeze at each pick point?

Show the answer

Answer: C. 4,619 lb

Each leg holds W ÷ 2 = 8,000 lb up; squeeze = 8,000 ÷ tan 60° = 8,000 ÷ 1.732 = 4,619 lb

Each leg holds up half, 8,000 lb. Divide by the tangent of 60, 1.732.

4,619 lb. It's C. A is just half the load. B is the sling tension, not the squeeze. D multiplies by the tangent instead of dividing.

C. Mechanical power transmission 32 of 135 exam questions (24 %)

QUESTION 29 OF 30

A deep groove ball bearing is stamped 6208. What is its bore diameter?

Show the answer

Answer: B. 40 mm

Last two digits = bore code 08; codes 04 to 96: bore = code × 5 → 08 × 5 = 40 mm

The last two digits are the bore code. Here that's 08. For codes from 04 up to 96, you multiply the code by 5. 08 times 5 is 40 mm. The first digits tell you the bearing type and series, not the size of the bore.

A read the code as the size. C read the type and series digits as the size. D read the last three digits. Below 04, the codes are fixed values, so learn those four: 10, 12, 15 and 17 mm.

E. Fluid power systems 21 of 135 exam questions (16 %)

QUESTION 30 OF 30

A hydraulic cylinder has a 4 in bore. The relief valve is set at 1,500 psi. Can it push a 20,000 lbf load?

Show the answer

Answer: C. No: it can push only about 18,850 lbf

A = π × 4² ÷ 4 = 12.566 in²; F = P × A = 1,500 × 12.566 ≈ 18,850 lbf < 20,000 lbf → it stalls at relief

Force is pressure times area. First, the area of the piston. π times the bore squared, divided by 4. 4² is 16. Times π, divided by 4, is 12.566 in². Now the force. 1,500 lb on every square inch, times 12.566, is about 18,850 lb. That's the most it can push before the relief opens. 20,000 is more than that.

The cylinder stalls and the oil goes over the relief. A squared the bore but forgot to divide by 4. B used the bore as if it were the radius. D multiplied by the bore, not the area. Area is where most hydraulics marks are lost. Work it out first, every time.
