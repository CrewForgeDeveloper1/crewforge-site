Turning and flipping loads: over centre, catch lines and tailing

Halfway through a turn the hoist line goes slack and the load flops onto its side. Millwrights turn loads all the time: a gearbox rolled over for a seal change, a pump base flipped to weld the bottom, a column stood up from horizontal. The Red Seal exam asks what the hoist carries during a turn, when the load goes over centre, and how two hooks share a load. Rules differ by province, so check yours.

The hoist and the pivot share the load

Roll an 8,000 lb crate, 4 ft wide and 6 ft tall, over one bottom edge, the pivot, with the hoist on a lug at the far bottom edge. Before it moves, the centre of gravity is 2 ft from the pivot and the hoist is 4 ft away. Take moments about the pivot: the hoist carries 8,000 × 2 ÷ 4 = 4,000 lb, half the weight. The pivot edge carries the rest. You're not lifting the crate, you're tipping it.

As it turns, the centre of gravity moves closer to the pivot. At 20° it is 0.853 ft from the pivot and the hoist line is 3.759 ft away, so the hoist holds 8,000 × 0.853 ÷ 3.759, about 1,816 lb. The further it turns, the less the hoist holds.

Over centre: where the hoist lets go

The load goes over centre when its centre of gravity is straight above the pivot. The tangent of that angle is the width over the height. For the 4 ft by 6 ft crate, 4 ÷ 6 gives 33.7°. At that instant it balances on its edge and the hoist holds nothing. One degree more and it wants to fall the other way, and a hoist line can only pull. A tall, narrow load goes over sooner: a box 3 ft wide and 6 ft tall goes over at 26.6°. A low, wide pump base 4 ft long and 2 ft high goes over late, at 63.4°.

Keep booming up with only the bottom lug rigged and the crate goes over centre, the hoist line goes slack and the crate flops onto its side, faster and faster. Anyone on that side, or any hand on the load, is in its path.

Catch lines and top-corner lugs

Rig a catch line to the top corner on the side the load turns toward, and keep it just snug before over centre. When the load passes over centre, the hoist goes slack and the catch line takes over. On the 8,000 lb crate it holds about 2,460 lb at 60°, and half the weight, 4,000 lb, at 90° just before the crate lands. On a crane that means two hooks, or one hook and a tugger or chain hoist on the catch side. On the shop floor, a second chain hoist or a come-along does the job. Pad the pivot edge with a timber, have one person signal, and keep everyone off the side it turns toward.

Or hook the hoist to a lug at the top far corner. The hoist line and the centre of gravity then sit on the same diagonal from the pivot, so the hoist holds half the weight the whole way and stays tight past over centre. The hook has to follow the lug sideways to stay plumb; a line dragged off plumb side-loads the crane. A lug only halfway up still goes slack at over centre. Anything lower than the top corner needs a catch line.

Tailing a long load with two hooks

To stand a column up, the main hook takes the top end and a tail hook holds the bottom end so it never drags. A 10,000 lb column, 16 ft long, lying flat puts 5,000 lb on each hook. At 40° the main has 5,365 lb and the tail 4,635 lb. Near vertical the tail unloads fast, and standing straight up the main hook has all 10,000 lb. Only then do you unhook the tail. The main crane must be rated for the whole weight.

In British Columbia a lift with two cranes is a tandem lift, and if either crane is loaded past 75 % of its rating it is a critical lift with a written lift plan (OHS Regulation Part 14). Check your province.

The formula

Over centre when tan φ = width ÷ height
Hoist = W × x_G ÷ x_A (moments about the pivot)
A low lug goes slack at over centre: rig a catch line high on the far side
A top-corner lug holds W ÷ 2 the whole way (keep the hook plumb)
Tailing: tail unloads as it stands up; main ends with 100 %

The load goes over centre when its centre of gravity is straight above the pivot: tan φ = width ÷ height. A box 3 ft wide and 6 ft tall: 3 ÷ 6 = 0.5, so 26.6°. Past that angle a low lug goes slack, so plan the catch line first.

Worked exam questions

These questions are from the video. Try each one before you read the solution.

WORKED EXAMPLE 1

A box 3 ft wide and 6 ft tall, centre of gravity in the middle, is tipped over one bottom edge. At what angle does it go over centre?

  • A. 26.6°
  • B. 63.4°
  • C. 45°
  • D. 30°

Answer: A. 26.6°

It goes over when the centre of gravity is straight above the pivot. The tangent of the angle is the width over the height. 3 over 6, one half.

Over centre when the CG is above the pivot: tan φ = width ÷ height = 3 ÷ 6 = 0.5 → φ = 26.6°

26.6°. A. B turns it upside down. A tall narrow load goes over sooner than you think.

WORKED EXAMPLE 2

A 10,000 lb column is upended with a main crane on the top end and a tail crane on the bottom end. Once the column hangs vertical, what does the main crane carry?

  • A. 5,000 lb
  • B. 10,000 lb
  • C. 7,500 lb
  • D. 0 lb

Answer: B. 10,000 lb

As the column stands up, the tail hook's share falls. Straight up, the column hangs from the main hook alone.

At vertical the tail line has no lever arm left: the main hook carries the whole 10,000 lb, so it must be rated for all of it

All 10,000 lb. B. A is what each carries lying flat. That's why the main crane is sized for the whole load.

Common mistakes

  • Thinking the hoist carries the whole load during a turn
  • Forgetting the over-centre angle: tan φ = width ÷ height
  • Turning past over centre with no catch line
  • Sizing the main crane for half the load when tailing
  • Unhooking the tail before the load is vertical

Take moments about the pivot, work out the over-centre angle before you lift, rig the catch line high on the far side, and size the main crane for the whole load when tailing.
