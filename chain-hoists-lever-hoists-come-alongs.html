Chain hoists, lever hoists and come-alongs: pull, ratio and pre-use checks

A 1-ton lever hoist with a pipe slipped over its handle can drop a load it should never have moved. The cheater bar doesn't make the hoist stronger: it hides the overload until the pawl lets go. The Red Seal exam asks how hand chain and lever hoists turn a small pull into a big lift, and what you check before every lift. The hoist figures on this page come from maker catalogues and manuals: always use the tag and manual for your own hoist.

Pull, overhaul ratio and efficiency

A hand chain hoist is a gear train. You pull the hand chain, it turns the hand wheel, and the gears turn the load sheave that lifts the load chain. The maker's table gives two numbers: the pull to lift the rated load, and the overhaul ratio, which is the feet of hand chain you pull for each foot of lift.

For a 1-ton (2,000 lb) Harrington CB hoist the pull is 58 lb and the overhaul ratio is 43. With no friction, 58 × 43 = 2,494 lb. The hoist lifts 2,000 lb, so it is about 80 % efficient (2,000 ÷ 2,494). Pull goes with the load: lifting 1,500 lb takes about 1,500 × 58 ÷ 2,000 = 44 lb, and lifting it 3 ft takes 3 × 43 = 129 ft of hand chain. Bigger hoists use more gearing and more chain falls: the 5-ton model needs 68 lb of pull, has an overhaul ratio of 198 and hangs the load on two falls, so each fall carries half.

Lever hoists, come-alongs and cheater bars

A lever hoist drives the same kind of load chain with a ratchet handle. It is short and light and works in any direction, so it is used for pulling and tensioning as well as lifting; most crews call it a come-along. The maker gives a rated lever pull: 72 lb on a 1-ton Harrington LB lifts the full ton. Needing more pull than that means the hoist is overloaded or needs repair. On a 1-1/2-ton hoist rated at 64 lb, needing 90 lb means the load is about 1.4 times the rating, roughly 4,200 lb.

Every lever hoist manual says the same: never use a cheater bar or handle extension, one person only, hand power only, a straight line from hook to hook, and never load the hoist in neutral (freewheel). A 4 ft pipe on a 9.6 in handle gives five times the leverage, so 65 lb at the hand drives a 9,000 lb load into a 1-ton hoist. Some wire rope ratchet pullers are also sold as come-alongs; many are labelled for pulling only, not lifting.

Overload clutches

Many hand chain and electric chain hoists have an overload clutch or load limiter. Above its setting the hand wheel or motor turns but the hook doesn't rise. It protects the hoist; it is not a scale, and you never use it to find out what a load weighs. A hoist without one will lift an overload with very little extra pull, so know the weight before you lift, and never put two people on one hand chain.

Pre-use checks

Check the hoist before every lift:

  • Pull × overhaul ratio × efficiency = lift
  • Pull goes with the load: more pull = more load
  • Lever hoists: no cheater bar, one person
  • Hooks: 5 % throat (maker), twist, latch
  • Chain: gauge over 5 pitches vs the discard
  • Test the brake; clamp rated ≥ hoist; no side pull

Hooks: no cracks, bends or twist, a latch that closes the throat, and a throat opening no more than the maker's limit over the new measurement (5 % for the hoists shown). Canadian rules point to the same number: BC removes a hook past 15 % or past the maker's criteria, whichever comes first (WorkSafeBC s.15.29), Alberta uses the maker's specifications (OHS Code s.309), and Ontario construction projects allow no opening past new (O. Reg. 213/91 s.173(4)). Check your province. A lever hoist hook that measured 34 mm new and now measures 36 mm has opened 5.9 %: replace it and find the overload. Load chain: no nicks, gouges, weld spatter, rust or twisted links. Gauge it over five pitches with a caliper and compare with the maker's discard length; a 6.3 mm chain that is 95.5 mm new and 98.6 mm now is past its 98.3 mm discard. Never shorten, weld or repair load chain.

Test the brake by taking a little load, stopping and watching before you go higher; on a hand chain hoist the pawl should click as the load rises. Hang the hoist from a beam clamp, trolley or lug rated for at least the hoist's capacity, and keep it right over the load: no side pulls.

The formula

Pull × overhaul ratio × efficiency = lift
Pull goes with the load: more pull = more load
Lever hoists: no cheater bar, one person
Hooks: 5 % throat (maker), twist, latch
Chain: gauge over 5 pitches vs the discard
Test the brake; clamp rated ≥ hoist; no side pull

Multiply the pull by the overhaul ratio and the efficiency to get the lift. Turned around, the pull for any load is the load divided by the rated capacity, times the pull at rated load. The hand chain you pull is the overhaul ratio times the lift.

Worked exam questions

These questions are from the video. Try each one before you read the solution.

WORKED EXAMPLE 1

A 5-ton hand chain hoist needs 68 lb of pull at its rated load. About how hard do you pull to lift 7,500 lb?

  • A. 34 lb
  • B. 68 lb
  • C. 51 lb
  • D. 91 lb

Answer: C. 51 lb

Pull goes with the load. Five tons is 10,000 lb. 7,500 over 10,000 is 0.75. Times 68 is 51 lb.

Pull ∝ load: 7,500 ÷ 10,000 × 68 = 51 lb

B is the pull at the full rated load. D divided the wrong way and got more pull for less load. And notice this hoist has two chain falls, so each fall carries half the load.

WORKED EXAMPLE 2

A 1-1/2-ton hand chain hoist needs 70 lb of pull at rated load and has an overhaul ratio of 57. It lifts a 2,400 lb gearbox 2 ft. How hard do you pull, and how much hand chain do you pull?

  • A. 56 lb and 114 ft
  • B. 70 lb and 57 ft
  • C. 56 lb and 57 ft
  • D. 88 lb and 114 ft

Answer: A. 56 lb and 114 ft

1.5 tons is 3,000 lb. 2,400 over 3,000 is 0.8. Times 70 is 56 lb. Then 2 ft of lift, times 57, is 114 ft of hand chain.

Pull = 2,400 ÷ 3,000 × 70 = 56 lb · hand chain = 2 ft × 57 = 114 ft

B is the rated pull and 1 ft of lift. C forgot the second foot. And D divided the wrong way.

Common mistakes

  • Cheater bar, or two people on the chain
  • Using the clutch as a scale
  • Loading a lever hoist in neutral
  • Side pulls and loads on the hook tip
  • Clamp or lug rated under the hoist
  • Repairing or lengthening load chain

Use the maker's rated pull: more pull than that is an overload, and a cheater bar or a second person only hides it.
