Gantries, A-frames and tripods: leg reactions, ratings, legs and feet

A 2-ton gantry lifting a 2-ton load can still tip over, if it stands on soft ground with the trolley pushed over to one leg. Nothing was over its rating: the ground was. The Red Seal exam asks how a load gets from the hook to the floor through a portable gantry, A-frame or tripod, and what can go wrong on the way. The worked examples ignore the gantry's own weight, as exam questions usually do; on a real lift, add it.

How the load splits between the legs

A gantry beam is a simply supported beam. With the load W at distance a from leg A and b from leg B (a + b = L, the span), the reactions are R_A = W × b ÷ L and R_B = W × a ÷ L. Each leg carries the load times the distance to the other leg, over the span, and the two always add back to the load.

On a 10 ft span with 2,000 lb at mid-span, each leg carries 1,000 lb. Roll the trolley to 3 ft from leg A and leg A carries 2,000 × 7 ÷ 10 = 1,400 lb while leg B carries 600 lb. At 1 ft from A, leg A carries 1,800 lb: almost the whole load sits on one end. On a 12 ft span with 3,000 lb 4 ft from leg A, leg A carries 3,000 × 8 ÷ 12 = 2,000 lb.

Ratings and the beam

A gantry and the hoist on it each have a rating, and the system is rated at the lower of the two. A 1-ton gantry with a 2-ton hoist is a 1-ton system. Gantry makers say to use a hoist rated the same as the gantry or lower, and the same rule covers anything a hoist hangs from (WorkSafeBC s.14.11(1)). The rating must be marked on the equipment (Ontario Reg. 851 s.51(1)(c); Alberta OHS Code s.59(4) and s.62), and a competent person examines a lifting device at least once a year in Ontario (Reg. 851 s.51(1)(b)).

The beam's bending moment under the load is W × a × b ÷ L, largest at mid-span where it is W × L ÷ 4. That's why a beam is rated with the load in the middle. A 3,000 lb load at the middle of a 10 ft beam makes 3,000 × 10 ÷ 4 = 7,500 ft·lb. Maker margins cover the hoist's weight and impact, not extra load.

Leaning legs: A-frames and tripods

Each end of an A-frame gantry is two legs leaning in to meet at the beam. Because they lean, each leg carries more than half the end load: C = R ÷ (2 × cos θ), with θ measured from vertical. An end carrying 1,400 lb with legs at 20° puts 1,400 ÷ (2 × 0.940) = 745 lb in each leg; at 30° it is about 808 lb. In metric, 1,400 kg at 25° is 1,400 ÷ (2 × 0.906) = 772 kg per leg.

A tripod shares the load three ways: C = W ÷ (3 × cos θ). An 1,800 lb load with legs 20° from vertical puts about 639 lb in each leg. Leaning legs also push outward at the feet (C × sin θ), so tie a tripod's feet; on a gantry the bottom tie or caster frame takes that push.

Feet and floors

Each end's load goes into the floor through its feet. Pressure is the foot load divided by the area. A 10 × 10 in plate is 100 in², or 0.694 ft², so 2,000 lb on it is 2,000 ÷ 0.694 = 2,880 psf. Compare that with what the ground can carry. The BC Building Code allows about 150 kPa (3,133 psf) on dense or compact gravel, but only 40 kPa (835 psf) on soft clay. On soft ground, spread the load with timber mats: a 24 × 24 in mat is 4 ft². On concrete, keep the floor level and lock the casters.

Other portable hoisting rules from the gantry makers:

  • Read the rating label; hoist ≤ gantry
  • Firm, level floor; casters locked; no slopes
  • Soft ground: plates and mats, check the pressure
  • Load centred and low when moving; push the gantry
  • No height or spread changes under load
  • Tie tripod feet; anchor rated ≥ hoist

The formula

R_A = W × b ÷ L (b = distance to the other leg)
Beam moment W × a × b ÷ L: worst at mid-span, W × L ÷ 4
System rating = the lower rating
A-frame leg: R ÷ (2 cos θ) · tripod leg: W ÷ (3 cos θ)
Pressure = foot load ÷ area: compare with the ground
Tie the feet; level, firm floor

Multiply the load by the distance to the other leg and divide by the span. The closer the load is to a leg, the more that leg carries. Then share each end's load among its feet and divide by the foot area to check the ground.

Worked exam questions

These questions are from the video. Try each one before you read the solution.

WORKED EXAMPLE 1

A gantry has a 12 ft span. A 3,000 lb load hangs 4 ft from leg A. Ignoring the gantry's own weight, what does leg A carry?

  • A. 1,000 lb
  • B. 1,500 lb
  • C. 2,000 lb
  • D. 3,000 lb

Answer: C. 2,000 lb

Use the distance to the other leg. 12 minus 4 is 8 ft. 3,000 times 8, over 12, is 2,000 lb. Leg B gets the other 1,000.

R_A = W × b ÷ L = 3,000 × 8 ÷ 12 = 2,000 lb (leg B: 1,000 lb)

A is leg B's share. That's the mistake of using the near distance instead of the far one. The closer the load is to a leg, the more that leg carries.

WORKED EXAMPLE 2

A 2-ton gantry, 10 ft span, sits on compact gravel (allowable 150 kPa, about 3,133 psf). A 3,000 lb pump hangs 2 ft from leg A. Each end has two feet on 10 in × 10 in plates. Ignoring the gantry's weight, what is the pressure under each foot at leg A, and is it OK?

  • A. 1,728 psf: OK
  • B. 3,456 psf: too high
  • C. 432 psf: OK
  • D. 12 psf: OK

Answer: A. 1,728 psf: OK

First the reaction. 3,000 times 8, over 10, is 2,400 lb at end A. The pair of plates shares it: 1,200 lb each. The plate is 0.694 ft². 1,200 over 0.694 is about 1,728 lb/ft². That's under the allowable.

R_A = 3,000 × 8 ÷ 10 = 2,400 lb → 1,200 lb a foot; 1,200 ÷ 0.694 ft² = 1,728 psf < 3,133 psf: OK

B forgot that each end stands on a pair of plates. C used the distance to the near leg. And D is pounds per square inch, labelled as square feet.

Common mistakes

  • Using the near distance for the reaction
  • Trusting the hoist's rating instead of the gantry's
  • Forgetting cos θ on leaning legs
  • Pounds per square inch vs per square foot
  • Casters or small plates on soft ground
  • Tripod feet left untied

Use the distance to the far leg, the lower of the two ratings, cos θ for leaning legs, and watch psi versus psf.
