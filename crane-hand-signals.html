Crane hand signals: the standard signals and who can stop a lift

A load is swinging toward a worker. The signaller is looking the other way. You see it. Can you stop the crane? Yes. On most sites the operator can't see everything, so the signaller is the operator's eyes. Get a signal wrong and the crane does exactly what you told it to.

One signaller, in plain view

The signaller stands where the operator can see them, with a clear view of the load. That is the line of sight. One person gives the signals at a time. Two people waving at the operator is how loads get dropped.

There is one exception. Anyone who sees danger can call a stop, and the operator must obey it. If a labourer sees a worker walk under the load and gives the emergency stop, the operator stops at once and sorts it out after. The signals here follow the American OSHA chart. Most match the Canadian charts, but stop and emergency stop differ: Ontario's IHSA chart and WorkSafeBC show one stop signal (one arm, palm down, hand moving), and CCOHS shows emergency stop with one arm. Use your site's chart and agree on it before the lift.

Finger for the load line

Hoist is the forearm straight up, index finger up, making small circles. The hook comes up. Lower is the arm down, index finger pointing down, making small circles. The hook goes down.

Before those, the signaller can pick the line. A fist tapped on top of the head means the main hoist. A tap on the elbow means the whip line, the auxiliary hoist. Tap the elbow and then give hoist, and the ball on the whip line comes up, not the main hook.

Thumb for the boom

Boom signals use the thumb. Arm out, fist closed, thumb up: raise the boom, and the radius gets shorter. Thumb down: lower the boom, and the radius gets longer.

Two combined signals add the fingers. Thumb up with the fingers opening and closing means raise the boom and lower the load: the load comes in at the same height. Thumb down with the fingers working means lower the boom and raise the load: the load goes out at the same height.

This is where one wrong hand tips a crane. The load needs to come down, so the signal is a finger pointing down. The signaller gives a thumb down instead. The boom comes down, the radius grows past the chart and the crane tips. A thumb and a finger look alike from across a site. Give every signal clearly. If the operator isn't sure, the operator stops.

Swing, telescope and move slowly

For swing, the arm goes out and the index finger points the way the boom should go. To swing the other way, the other arm points. Telescoping booms have their own pair: both fists in front of the body, thumbs pointing out, means extend the boom. Thumbs pointing toward each other means retract. A longer boom or a longer radius means less capacity, so telescoping out with a load on is a chart check, not just a signal.

To move slowly, one hand gives the signal and the other hand is held still in front of it. The motion runs at a crawl, about a third of normal speed. It works with any motion signal. Use it for the last few inches as the load lands.

Stop, emergency stop, dog everything

Stop is one arm out level, palm down, swinging back and forth. Every motion stops. Emergency stop, on the OSHA chart, is both arms out, palms down, back and forth. Everything stops at once. Dog everything is the hands clasped in front at the waist. All motions stop, the brakes are set, and the crane holds until the signaller starts again.

Before the lift, and when you can't see

Ontario's construction regulation (O. Reg. 213/91) says the operator and the signaller set the procedures together before they start. Agree who the signaller is, agree on the signals, and agree that anyone can call stop. Under the same regulation, a signaller is needed when the operator can't see the load's path or someone could be hurt. The signaller is a competent worker who does no other work while signalling and wears a fluorescent orange garment with yellow reflective stripes. Other provinces are similar, so check yours.

When hand signals won't work, use radios. British Columbia's regulation says so directly. The channel is for the lift only, and the operator's radio is hands-free. With more than one crane, name the crane before the command. One voice still gives the signals, and a stop from anyone is still obeyed.

The formula

Finger + circles: hoist or lower
One signaller, in view, watching the load
Thumb: boom up or down
Stop · emergency stop (one arm or both) · dog everything
Anyone can stop it · no line of sight: radio

There is no maths in hand signals, but one rule sorts out most exam questions: the index finger moves the load line and the thumb moves the boom. Fingers opening and closing with a thumb signal move the load the opposite way to the boom. Stop is one arm, emergency stop is both arms, and dog everything is hands clasped.

Worked exam questions

These questions are from the video. Try each one before you read the solution.

WORKED EXAMPLE 1

During a lift, a labourer who is not the designated signaller sees a worker walk under the load and gives the emergency stop signal. What should the operator do?

  • A. Ignore it: only the designated signaller gives signals
  • B. Stop at once
  • C. Finish the motion, then stop
  • D. Radio the signaller to confirm first

Answer: B. Stop at once

One person gives the signals, with one exception. A stop, or an emergency stop, from anyone. The operator stops first and sorts it out after.

The operator obeys a stop signal from anyone (CCOHS; WorkSafeBC s.14.47(2); IHSA)

A is the rule for every other signal, and it's why the exam asks this one.

WORKED EXAMPLE 2

One arm out, thumb pointing down, and all four fingers opening and closing. What does the operator do?

  • A. Lower the boom
  • B. Hoist
  • C. Lower the boom and raise the load
  • D. Stop

Answer: C. Lower the boom and raise the load

Thumb down means the boom comes down. The fingers working mean the load line moves too, the other way. It hoists. Both at once: the boom lowers while the load line hoists, and the load moves out at about the same height.

Thumb down = boom down; fingers opening and closing = the load line works the other way (hoists): LOWER THE BOOM AND RAISE THE LOAD (the load goes out at about the same height)

A reads only the thumb. B reads only the fingers. Watch the thumb and the fingers, every time.

Common mistakes

  • Finger = load line · thumb = boom
  • Only the signaller can stop the crane (wrong)
  • Stop vs emergency stop vs dog everything
  • Two signallers at once
  • Hand signals with no line of sight

Finger is the load line, thumb is the boom, and a stop from anyone is obeyed.
