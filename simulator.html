Slings: hitches and sling angles

Pick a hitch, set the load and the sling angle, and watch the tension in each leg against the sling's rating. Try 60°, then 45°, then 30°, and see why a low sling angle is one of the most-tested traps on the Red Seal exam.

Open the simulator

Open full screen · Exam preparation only: always check the sling tag, the manufacturer's charts and your lift plan.

Exam tip: the angle in the formula is measured from the horizontal. If you're given the angle from vertical, or the included angle at the hook, convert it first.
Study the topic: Sling angle factor

What this simulator works out

The tension in each sling leg for a vertical hitch, a choker, a basket and a two- or four-leg bridle, from the load weight and the sling angle, against the sling's rating for that hitch. Wire rope, alloy chain or synthetic web, in pounds or kilograms.

The formula in words

Tension in each leg equals the load divided by the number of legs, times the load angle factor. The load angle factor is 1 divided by the sine of the sling angle, measured from the horizontal: 1.155 at 60°, 1.414 at 45° and 2.000 at 30°. Plan a four-leg bridle as if only two legs carry the load.

Worked example

  • Imperial: a 10,000 lb load on a two-leg bridle at 60°: 10,000 ÷ 2 = 5,000 lb, times 1.155 = 5,775 lb in each leg.
  • Metric: a 4,000 kg load on a two-leg bridle at 60°: 4,000 ÷ 2 = 2,000 kg, times 1.155 = 2,310 kg in each leg (about 22.7 kN).
