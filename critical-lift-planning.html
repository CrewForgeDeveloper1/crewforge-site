Critical lift planning: percent of chart, tandem lifts, wind and power lines

Every lift is planned. Most plans are short. Some lifts are critical, and those need a written plan, signed off, with a meeting before the lift. The Red Seal standard lists lift planning as part of the millwright's job. The crane and chart numbers on this page are an example made for training, not a real crane. Critical-lift rules differ by province, so check yours.

Every plan starts with the same numbers

Start with the load weight, from a drawing, a scale ticket or a calculation, never a guess. Add the hook block, the rigging and the chart deductions to get the gross load. Then find the loaded radius and the chart for your set-up. On our example crane (training only, not a real crane), a 10,350 lb gross load against a chart value of 23,200 lb is 44.6 % of the chart.

Percent of chart is the gross load divided by the chart capacity at the loaded radius. A 9,450 lb gross load against 13,500 lb is 70 %. Then work through the rest of the list: wind, power lines, people, the ground, the exclusion zone and the signaller. If nothing makes it critical, it's a routine lift. Routine still means planned.

What makes a lift critical

It depends on where you work. British Columbia spells it out in its OHS Regulation (s.14.1). Alberta's OHS Code (s.68.1) uses a different trigger.

  • BC: one crane > 90 % of chart beyond half its max radius
  • BC: tandem lift, either crane > 75 %
  • BC: more than two cranes
  • BC: people in a suspended work platform
  • BC: over or between energized high-voltage lines
  • Alberta: > 75 % of capacity → written lift calculation

Your province, the owner or the site can be stricter. Under the chart is not the same as routine.

The 90 % rule in action

Take one crane at 91.8 % of the chart, with a loaded radius of 39.6 ft. The longest radius on this boom's example chart is 60 ft, and half of that is 30 ft. The lift is over 90 % and past half the radius, so in British Columbia it is a critical lift: written plan, signed, with a meeting first.

Bring the load in to 25 ft and it drops to about 74 %. That is under both triggers, so it is routine there. In Alberta it is still under 75 %, so no lift calculation is needed, but only just.

Tandem lifts: shares come from moments

With two cranes on one load, each crane's share comes from the centre of gravity. Take a 30,000 lb beam with picks 30 ft apart and the centre of gravity 12 ft from pick A. Crane A carries the weight times the distance to the far pick, divided by the span: 30,000 × 18 ÷ 30 = 18,000 lb. Crane B gets the rest, 12,000 lb. The crane nearer the centre of gravity carries more.

Then add each crane's own hook block and rigging and check each crane against its own chart. Crane A carries 18,000 lb plus a 650 lb hook block and 150 lb of rigging: 18,800 lb against 26,700 lb on its example chart is 70.4 %. Crane B is at 47.9 %. Neither is over 75 %, so it is not critical by the BC tandem trigger. It still needs a plan both operators and the signaller follow.

Get the centre of gravity wrong and it goes badly. If the plan assumes the middle but the centre of gravity sits close to crane A, crane A's share climbs past its tipping load and crane A tips. A few feet of error moves thousands of pounds onto one crane.

Wind and power lines

A load is a sail. As the wind picks up, the load swings out, and a swinging load adds to the radius. The example chart says to reduce capacity above 20 mph, and many real charts have a note like that. At 28 mph you are past the limit: follow the maker's reduction or stop until the wind drops. Put the wind limits in the written plan, with who checks the wind and how.

In British Columbia, lifting over or between energized high-voltage lines is critical. Every province sets limits of approach. Ontario's construction regulation (O. Reg. 213/91, s.188) sets 3 m for lines from 750 V up to 150 kV, 4.5 m over 150 kV up to 250 kV, and 6 m over 250 kV. A 115 kV line falls in the 3 m band.

The written lift plan

  • Load weight, rigging, load sharing
  • Crane, set-up, radius, chart capacity
  • Wind limits, hoist and travel speeds
  • Signallers: who and where
  • Tandem critical lift: a supervisor not running a crane; documented pre-job meeting

British Columbia lists these for every critical lift. For a critical tandem lift, a qualified supervisor who is not running either crane directs it, after a pre-job meeting that is written down. Ontario adds the ground and blocking under the outriggers, and an exclusion zone.

The formula

% of chart = gross ÷ chart (loaded radius)
BC critical: > 90 % beyond ½ radius, tandem > 75 %, …
Alberta: > 75 % → lift calculation
Tandem: A = W × b ÷ span
Wind and power-line limits in the plan

Percent of chart is the gross load divided by the chart capacity at the loaded radius. In a tandem lift, crane A carries the weight times the distance from the centre of gravity to crane B's pick, divided by the span: A = W × b ÷ span. Crane B carries the rest. Add each crane's own hook block and rigging before you check its chart. The chart numbers used on this page come from an example chart made for training. It is not a real crane's chart.

Worked exam questions

These questions are from the video. Try each one before you read the solution.

WORKED EXAMPLE 1

A 30,000 lb beam is lifted by two cranes whose picks are 30 ft apart. The beam's centre of gravity is 12 ft from crane A's pick. What load does crane A carry, before its hook block and rigging?

  • A. 12,000 lb
  • B. 15,000 lb
  • C. 18,000 lb
  • D. 20,000 lb

Answer: C. 18,000 lb

The crane nearer the centre of gravity carries more. Crane A's share is the weight times the distance to the other pick, 18 ft, over the span. 30,000 times 18 over 30. 18,000 lb.

A = W × b ÷ span = 30,000 × (30 − 12) ÷ 30 = 18,000 lb (B = 12,000 lb)

A is crane B's share. B splits it in half, which only works when the centre of gravity is in the middle.

WORKED EXAMPLE 2

64 ft boom. Radius 38.5 ft before loading, 40.1 ft loaded. Gross load 18,350 lb. Chart (64 ft): 20,000 lb at 40 ft, 13,500 lb at 50 ft. Wind 12 mph (chart limit 20 mph). What is the verdict?

  • A. Routine: 91.8 % of the chart
  • B. Critical: over 90 % beyond half the radius
  • C. Do not lift: 135.9 % of the 50 ft row
  • D. Routine: under 20,000 lb

Answer: C. Do not lift: 135.9 % of the 50 ft row

The loaded radius is past 40, so you read the 50 ft row. 18,350 divided by 13,500. 135.9 %. Over the chart. Don't lift. Re-plan with more boom angle or a bigger crane.

Loaded radius 40.1 ft → 50 ft row: 18,350 ÷ 13,500 = 135.9 % → over the chart: do not lift; re-plan

B looked at the 40 ft row. It would be critical if the row were right, but the row is wrong.

Common mistakes

  • Under the chart ≠ routine
  • Tandem load split in half without the CG
  • Each crane's hook block and rigging forgotten
  • Lifting past the wind limit
  • Another province's triggers

Find the centre of gravity before you split a tandem load, and use your own province's triggers.
