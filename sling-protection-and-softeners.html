Sling protection: sharp edges, pads, softeners and sleeves

A quarter-inch web sling over a square steel corner can be cut at a fraction of the rating on its tag. Every sling bears on something: a beam flange, a plate edge, the corner of a machine base. Millwrights choose and fit the protection, and the Red Seal exam asks what makes an edge sharp, what each kind of protection really does, and when a damaged sling comes out of service.

When is an edge sharp?

SpanSet, quoting the German rigging rules, calls an edge sharp when its radius r is less than the thickness t of the sling. A web sling 1/4 in thick over a corner with a 1/16 in radius gives r ÷ t = 0.0625 ÷ 0.25 = 0.25: less than 1, so the edge is sharp. Round the corner to 1/4 in and r ÷ t = 1: not sharp by that test. For a wire rope sling, compare the radius with the rope diameter: Dolezych says an edge is always sharp if its radius is smaller than the rope.

Don't trust your eyes. Lift-All says edges do not need to be sharp to cause failure of the sling, and Columbus McKinnon puts it simply: if the edge can damage your hand, it can damage a sling. The clean, square edge of a steel beam or plate counts. Lift-All's minimum radius for unprotected two-ply webbing is 1/2 in.

Wear pads, corner softeners and cut-resistant sleeves

They do three different jobs. A wear pad stops abrasion on rough surfaces; a thin, soft pad takes the shape of the corner, so the radius stays small and a sharp edge still cuts. Unirope and Slingmax point out that abrasion and cutting are two different injuries that need different protection.

A corner softener holds its own radius, so the sling bends around the softener instead of the steel. A 1 in softener under a 1/4 in sling gives r ÷ t = 4: not sharp. A cut-resistant sleeve does not round the edge; it keeps the edge from reaching the sling, up to its maker's rating (Slingmax rates its CornerMax pads at 25,000 lb of protection per inch of sling width, a maker's claim for its own product).

Grinding a chamfer is not rounding: Lift-All says chamfering or cutting off edges is not an acceptable substitute for fully rounding them. And secure the protection. SpanSet says a protector only works if it is correctly attached and can't slip, because a tightening sling drags a loose pad off the edge.

Wire rope over an edge

On an edge, wire rope bends, and the bend costs strength. Taking the bend diameter as twice the edge radius (D = 2r, our geometry for an edge), a 1/2 in rope over a 1 in radius has D/d = 2 ÷ 0.5 = 4, and the Lift-It table keeps 75 %: a sling tagged 5,000 lb is good for 3,750 lb over that edge. A 3 in radius gives D/d = 12; use the next lower printed ratio, 10:1 = 86 %, for 4,300 lb. In metric, 16 mm rope over an 80 mm radius gives D/d = 160 ÷ 16 = 10, so a 2,000 kg sling is good for 1,720 kg.

On a square edge (r less than d) the rope kinks for good. Unirope warns that a sharp steel edge will cut through any wire rope sling.

The rules and removal from service

WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation s. 15.39: when a sling is applied to a sharp edge of a load, the edge or the sling must be protected. Alberta's OHS Code s. 296 says the same, and Ontario's construction regulation (O. Reg. 213/91 s. 172(3)) bars a web or nylon sling where it may be cut. Check your province.

A web sling comes out of service when an edge cut is longer than the web is thick (WorkSafeBC s. 15.54, Alberta s. 305) or abrasion is deeper than 15 % of the webbing thickness (BC). Red core yarn showing means removal too, but a cut may not show red (Lift-All). Wire rope comes out for kinks, crushing, bird-caging or other distortion. Always check the tag, the manufacturer's data and the lift plan: this is exam prep, not a procedure.

The formula

Wire rope over an edge: D = 2r, rating × D/d efficiency; a sharp edge kinks it
Sharp: r < t (synthetic), r < d (wire rope). If it can hurt your hand, it can hurt a sling
Wear pad = abrasion · softener = radius · cut-resistant sleeve = stops the cut (maker's rating)
A chamfer is not rounding; secure protection so it can't slip
Remove: any cut, tear, snag or hole in a web sling, red yarn, kinks or crushing

Sharp when r < t (synthetic sling) or r < d (wire rope). Wire rope over an edge: D = 2r, rating = tag WLL × the D/d efficiency. 1/2 in rope on a 2 in softener: D/d = 4 ÷ 0.5 = 8 → 84 %, 5,000 × 0.84 = 4,200 lb.

Worked exam questions

These questions are from the video. Try each one before you read the solution.

WORKED EXAMPLE 1

A two-ply web sling about 3/8 in thick goes over a plate edge ground to a 1/4 in radius. Using the rule r ≥ t, is the edge sharp?

  • A. No: the corner has been rounded
  • B. Yes: r ÷ t = 0.67, less than 1
  • C. No: 1/4 in is more than 3/16 in
  • D. Only if it has a burr

Answer: B. Yes: r ÷ t = 0.67, less than 1

Divide the radius by the thickness. A quarter over 3 eighths is about 0.67. Less than one.

r ÷ t = 0.25 ÷ 0.375 = 0.67 < 1 → sharp. Protect the edge or the sling.

It's sharp. B. A is the trap: a rounded corner can still be sharp for a thick sling. C uses one maker's figure for a one ply sling. D waits for damage you can see.

WORKED EXAMPLE 2

A 5/8 in wire rope sling tagged 7,000 lb bends over an edge with a 2-1/2 in radius. Taking D = 2r and the D/d efficiency table (8:1 = 84 %, 10:1 = 86 %), what is it good for over that edge?

  • A. 7,000 lb
  • B. 5,880 lb
  • C. 5,250 lb
  • D. 6,020 lb

Answer: B. 5,880 lb

D is twice the radius, 5 in. 5 over 5 eighths is 8. 8 : 1 keeps 84 %. 7,000 times 0.84.

D = 2 × 2.5 = 5 in; D/d = 5 ÷ 0.625 = 8 → 84 %; 7,000 × 0.84 = 5,880 lb

5,880 lb. B. A ignores the bend. C uses the radius as D, so 4 : 1. D reads the 10 : 1 row.

Common mistakes

  • Calling an edge "not sharp" because it looks round: test r against t
  • Using a wear pad on a sharp edge
  • Grinding a chamfer instead of rounding or protecting
  • Leaving protection loose, so it slips off
  • Forgetting the D/d loss for wire rope, or waiting for red yarn

Test the radius against the sling, never put a wear pad on a sharp edge, don't chamfer instead of rounding, secure the protection, and don't wait for red yarn to pull a cut sling.
