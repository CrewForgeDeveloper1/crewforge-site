Laser shaft alignment training and certification in Canada

Laser alignment is core millwright work. You'll see "certified" on a lot of course ads, so here is what actually exists in Canada, and what it's worth.

Checked as of November 2026. Course details change often. Check with the provider before you book.

What it is and who it's for

Shaft alignment puts two coupled shafts on one centre line while the machine runs. A laser system measures offset and angle across the coupling, then tells you how much to shim and move each foot. Good alignment cuts vibration, bearing wear and seal leaks. Every millwright needs it.

Is there a recognized certification?

We found no recognized Canadian certification for laser shaft alignment. There is no provincial licence and no Canadian body that certifies alignment on its own. What you'll find instead:

TypeExampleWhat you get
Maker or distributor courseTool makers and their distributors run alignment courses on their own equipment. One Fluke-listed PRUFTECHNIK course (held outside Canada) runs two days. A certificate of completion, not a certification.
Canadian training companyLaurentide course 4002, Shaft Alignment: 4 hours virtual or 8 hours in person, no prerequisites, price on request.Course training. Certificate type not stated.
US credentialSACA C-302 Laser Shaft Alignment 1, offered through some US college programs (US Department of Labor listing). We could not confirm details on SACA's own site. Check current details with SACA.
Part of a wider credentialVibration analyst categories (ISO 18436-2) cover alignment as one topic. See our vibration analyst guide.

Required or voluntary?

Voluntary. Your Red Seal is the trade credential. Some employers send crews on a maker course when they buy a new laser system.

Prerequisites, exam and cost

Maker courses usually have no prerequisites and no formal exam. Prices are rarely published, so ask for a quote. Certificates of completion don't expire, but your skill does. Practise.

How it connects to the Red Seal exam

Shaft alignment is task C-16 in the Red Seal exam weightings. Major work activity C, power transmission, is 22 % of the exam, and C-16 is 16 % of C. That is about five questions out of 135 (our estimate), on rough, dial and laser alignment. Dial methods are still tested, so learn the maths, not just the laser screen.

Practice questions

Written by CrewForge for practice. Not taken from any certification exam.

1. The motor shaft is 0.004 in high at the coupling and slopes up 0.5 thou per inch away from it. The front feet are 8 in from the coupling and the back feet 20 in. How much do you shim at each?

Remove 0.008 in at the front feet and 0.014 in at the back feet. Height at a foot = offset + slope × distance. Front: 0.004 + 0.0005 × 8 = 0.008 in. Back: 0.004 + 0.0005 × 20 = 0.014 in. The motor is high, so shims come out.

2. Convert the back-foot correction above to millimetres.

About 0.36 mm. 0.014 in × 25.4 = 0.356 mm.

3. What do you check and fix before you take alignment readings?

Soft foot. If one foot doesn't sit flat, tightening it bends the frame and your readings change. Check each foot with a dial or feeler gauge and correct it first.

4. At running temperature the pump grows 0.002 in and the motor grows 0.006 in. How should you set the motor cold?

0.004 in low. The motor will rise 0.004 in more than the pump. Set it that much low so the shafts line up when hot. Use the maker's thermal growth figures on the job.
