Signal person duties and radio protocol: blind lifts and lost communication

In the middle of a blind lift the radio goes quiet. In a blind lift the operator can't see the load, and every move the crane makes comes from one voice on the radio. Millwrights rig and land loads in pits, behind walls and inside buildings, so you will be that voice, or stand next to it. The Red Seal exam asks who signals, who can stop the crane, and what happens when communication is lost. Rules differ by province, so check yours.

One signal person, and a stop from anyone

When a wall stands between the cab and the landing, the operator works only on the directions of one signal person who can see the load and the landing. In British Columbia that's OHS Regulation s. 14.47: the operator acts only on the directions of a qualified signaller with a clear view. Ontario's construction and industrial regulations say the same in their own words. When hand signals won't work, because of distance, a wall or the weather, the signals go by radio.

Stop is the exception. Every other signal comes from the one designated signaller, but BC says the operator must stop on a stop signal from any person, and Alberta says the same. If a rigger who isn't the signaller sees someone walk under the load and signals stop, the operator stops.

The signal person's job

In Ontario the signaller is a competent worker who does no other work while signalling. Alberta adds that the employer designates the signaller, and only one designated signaller gives signals at a time. The signal person stands clear of the load's path, with a clear view of it, and watches the part of the path the operator can't see. In Alberta, before any proceed signal, the signaller makes sure there are no hazards in the area. Ontario wants the signaller in full view of the operator where practical, and otherwise another means of clear, direct communication: the radio. On an Ontario construction project the signaller wears a fluorescent blaze or international orange garment with reflective stripes. The words differ by province; the job doesn't.

Radio protocol

Before the lift, the operator and the signal person agree the words and the procedure; Ontario writes that into the regulation. Do a radio check from the spot where the signal person will actually stand. Use a channel for this crane only: BC doesn't allow multi-channel radios to direct tower cranes, the US rule asks for a dedicated channel, and another crew's voice on your channel can move your crane. The operator needs both hands on the controls, so the radio is hands-free, a headset.

The US rule gives each call an order: the function (boom, hoist), the direction, the distance or speed, then the function and stop. Boom down, slow, about ten feet. Boom, stop. Canada's regulations don't list an order, so your site's procedure does. During every move, keep talking, a voice every second or two, so the operator knows the link is alive.

Silence means stop

Every rule book lands in the same place: no word, no move. The operator stops and stays stopped until communication is back and a radio check proves it. BC requires effective communication to be established and maintained in tandem lifts. Alberta says that if signals can't be passed properly, the employer provides more signallers or another clear means. The US rule says the operator stops until communication is re-established; Canadian regulations don't use those exact words, so your lift plan does.

Even a good operator doesn't stop instantly. Distance is speed times time. At 1.1 ft/s, about a second to notice the silence moves the load about 1.1 ft, and easing off the lever adds a little more, about 1.2 ft in all. At 2.8 ft/s, the one second of reaction alone is 2.8 ft, and with about 0.45 s more to stop it's 2.8 × 1.45 = 4.06 ft. That's why the signal person keeps talking, and why you leave room.

The formula

No word, no move; distance = speed × time
Blind lift: the operator works on one signal person
Stop from anyone; every other signal from one person
Radio check, one channel, hands-free, agreed words
Function, direction, distance or speed, stop

The load keeps moving while the operator reacts and the crane stops: distance = speed × time. At 2.8 ft/s with 1.0 s to react and 0.45 s to stop: 2.8 × 1.45 = 4.06 ft. Add the times before you multiply.

Worked exam questions

These questions are from the video. Try each one before you read the solution.

WORKED EXAMPLE 1

During a lift, a rigger who is not the designated signaller sees a worker walk under the load and gives the stop signal. What does the operator do?

  • A. Ignore it: take signals only from the designated signaller
  • B. Stop: a stop signal is obeyed from anyone
  • C. Radio the signaller to ask
  • D. Finish the move, then stop

Answer: B. Stop: a stop signal is obeyed from anyone

The operator takes every other signal from one designated signaller. Stop is the exception. British Columbia says the operator must stop on a stop signal from any person. Alberta says the same.

BC s.14.47(2): the operator must stop on a stop signal from any person. Alberta s.191(7): the operator takes a STOP from any worker.

Stop, from anyone. A is the rule for every other signal. C and D lose the seconds that matter.

WORKED EXAMPLE 2

A load is moving toward a landing at 2.8 ft/s. The radio goes quiet and the operator takes 1.0 s to react. How far does the load move before the operator even starts to stop?

  • A. 0.36 ft
  • B. 1.4 ft
  • C. 2.8 ft
  • D. 28 ft

Answer: C. 2.8 ft

Distance is speed times time. 2.8 ft a second, times 1 second.

Distance = speed × time = 2.8 ft/s × 1.0 s = 2.8 ft (braking adds more after that)

2.8 ft. It's C. And braking adds more on top. A divides instead of multiplying. D slips a decimal.

Common mistakes

  • Ignoring a stop from someone who isn't the signaller
  • Two people giving signals
  • Keeping on moving when the radio goes quiet
  • Skipping the radio check
  • Forgetting distance = speed × time

Take a stop from anyone, keep to one signaller for every other signal, do the radio check on a channel for this crane only, and stop the moment the radio goes quiet.
