Roundslings and web slings: maker tables, EN mode factors, damage and edges

A web sling with a wear pad on the corner can still be cut through under a load well below its rating. Synthetic slings are light and kind to finished surfaces, but they're easy to damage. The Red Seal exam asks how to read their ratings, when they come out of service and how to protect them. This goes deeper than the synthetic slings basics: makers' tables, the European system, the removal rules and edge protection. Rules differ by province, so check yours.

Read the maker's table: it rounds down

In the North American colour code, a purple polyester roundsling is rated 2,600 lb vertical. A choker is 80 % of vertical: 2,080 lb by arithmetic, and Lift-All's table prints 2,100. A basket with the legs straight up is twice vertical, 5,200 lb. At 45° the formula, two times the sine of the angle, gives about 3,677 lb, but the maker prints 3,600. On angled baskets the tables round down, so read the table when you have it.

Green is 5,300 lb vertical and yellow 8,400 lb, and the colours go on up through tan, red, white, blue, orange and grey. A yellow roundsling in a choker is 8,400 × 0.8 = 6,720 lb, printed as 6,700. The colour gets you close; the tag tells you the truth.

European slings: mode factors, not sines

A sling built to EN 1492 is rated in kilograms and uses a design factor of 7, not 5. A blue label means polyester. A green EN web sling is 2,000 kg straight and 4,000 kg in a parallel basket (factor 2.0). The European system doesn't use the sine. It uses a mode factor for a range of angles: with the legs up to 45° from vertical the basket factor is 1.4, so 2,800 kg; from 45° to 60° it's 1.0, so 2,000 kg. Past 60° from vertical the tables give no rating at all.

Mind the angle reference: 30° from vertical is 60° from horizontal. A yellow EN sling, 3,000 kg straight, in a basket with the legs 30° from vertical is 3,000 × 1.4 = 4,200 kg. Using the North American 2 × sin formula on it would give the wrong answer, 5,196 kg.

Edges: a wear pad is not cut protection

Put a 2 in two-ply web sling, rated 6,200 lb vertical, in a basket at 60° around a steel box with a strip of old webbing on each corner. The basket is good for about 10,740 lb and the load is 6,000 lb, but the corner works through the pad, then through the sling. A wear pad stops rubbing. It isn't made to stop a square corner under load. A US Department of Energy alert put it plainly: there's no such thing as cut-proof protection. Match the protection to the edge and the load, and set it where the sling will bear once the load is up. In a Queensland crane drop, the protection was too thin or had moved.

A common maker rule: an edge is sharp when its radius is less than the sling's thickness. Lift-All's minimum radii for unprotected web slings are 3/16 in for one ply, 1/2 in for two ply, 3/4 in for three ply and 1 in for four ply. A chamfer is not rounding; it leaves two new sharp edges. BC's OHS Regulation (s. 15.39) says protect the edge or the sling, and Alberta's OHS Code (s. 296) says guard sharp edges.

When it comes out of service

BC's s. 15.54 takes a web sling out for an edge cut longer than the thickness of the webbing, wear that removes more than 15 % of the thickness, melting, charring, acid or caustic burns, broken or worn stitches in load-bearing splices, damaged end fittings, or any mix of smaller damage with the same effect. That's the legal minimum; makers such as Lift-All remove a sling for any cut, tear or snag, so follow the stricter rule. Lift-All puts red yarns in the core of its webbing: if red shows, it's out, but treat that as the last warning, not the only one. A roundsling with a cut cover and core yarns showing is out too.

Sunlight breaks down the fibres even when the sling looks fine. Lift-All takes one- and two-ply web slings out after about two years of total outdoor exposure, three years for three- and four-ply. Chemicals matter: Lift-All says polyester and alkalis don't mix, so a polyester roundsling is the wrong choice in a wash bay with a caustic cleaner, while nylon is rated fine with alkalis. Check the maker's chemical chart. And under Alberta's s. 305 a rejected sling must be altered so nobody can use it again. Cut it up.

The formula

EN basket: 2.0 parallel, 1.4 to 45°, 1.0 to 60° from vertical
Maker's table first: they round down
Sharp: edge radius under the sling thickness
BC 15.54: cut longer than the thickness, 15 % wear
Rejected: alter it so it can't be used

On an EN 1492 sling, multiply the straight rating by the mode factor for the angle from vertical: 2.0 for a parallel basket, 1.4 up to 45°, 1.0 up to 60°. A 3,000 kg sling with legs 30° from vertical: 3,000 × 1.4 = 4,200 kg. Don't use 2 × sin on a European sling.

Worked exam questions

These questions are from the video. Try each one before you read the solution.

WORKED EXAMPLE 1

A yellow EN 1492 web sling is rated 3,000 kg straight. In a basket with both legs 30° from vertical, what is its capacity?

  • A. 6,000 kg
  • B. 5,196 kg
  • C. 4,200 kg
  • D. 3,000 kg

Answer: C. 4,200 kg

On a European sling, use the mode factor. Legs within 45° of vertical, 1.4. 3,000 times 1.4 is 4,200 kg.

EN mode factor, basket β 0–45° = 1.4: 3,000 × 1.4 = 4,200 kg

A is the parallel basket. B used the North American sine formula on a European sling. D is straight.

WORKED EXAMPLE 2

Lift-All lists a minimum edge radius for using its web slings without protection. For a 2-ply web sling, what is it?

  • A. 3/16 in
  • B. 1/2 in
  • C. 1 in
  • D. Any edge is fine below the WLL

Answer: B. 1/2 in

Lift-All's table: three sixteenths for one ply, a half inch for two ply, ¾ for three ply, an inch for four ply. Thicker sling, bigger radius.

Lift-All Webmaster: 1-ply 3/16 in, 2-ply 1/2 in, 3-ply 3/4 in, 4-ply 1 in

A is 1 ply. C is 4 ply. And D is how slings get cut. Other makers have their own tables, so read the one for your sling.

Common mistakes

  • Using 2 × sin on a European sling
  • Trusting a wear pad on a square corner
  • Treating red yarns as the only warning
  • Reading the formula, not the maker's table
  • Keeping a rejected sling

Read the maker's table before the formula, use mode factors on European slings, fit cut protection rated for the edge, and cut up any sling you reject.
