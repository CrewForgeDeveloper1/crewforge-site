Overhead crane pre-use inspection: limit test, brake, hooks, rope and lockout

Skip one test at the start of a shift and an overhead crane can drive its hook block into the drum, part the rope and drop the block. That test is the no-load check of the upper limit switch. The Red Seal exam asks what you check before using a crane, what you do when something fails, and how a crane is locked out for work. Rules on this page are paraphrased from Canadian regulations (WorkSafeBC, Ontario and Alberta); provinces differ, so check yours. CSA B167 is cited by number only.

Know the crane, and its rating

A top running bridge crane has a bridge that spans the building and runs on the runway rails on its end trucks, a trolley that runs across the bridge, and a hoist on the trolley: motor, gearbox, brake and rope drum, reeved down to the hook block. A monorail hoist runs on one fixed beam with no bridge. The operator runs it from a pendant with a stop button on top, and the main switch is on the wall where it can be locked off.

The rated capacity is marked on the crane where the operator can read it from the floor (usually both sides of the bridge), and on the hoist and its load block (WorkSafeBC s.14.5; Ontario Reg. 851 s.51(1)(c); Alberta OHS Code s.62). No marking, or a marking you can't read, means you don't use the crane.

The start-of-shift check

Before use, the operator confirms the crane was inspected for that shift and its controls and safety devices were tested (WorkSafeBC s.14.35):

  • Capacity marked on both sides, and on the hoist or block
  • Every shift: upper limit, no load, slowly
  • Brake: light load just off the floor
  • Hooks, rope, pendant, horn, travel
  • Defect: record, report, tag out
  • Lockout: off, open, lock, tag, signs, rail stops

The upper limit switch is tried at the start of each shift with no load on the hook, raising the block slowly (WorkSafeBC s.14.55). It is a safety device, never an operating control (Ontario Reg. 851 s.51(5), unless it is designed for that and backed by a second limit switch). If it doesn't trip, the block can two-block: it runs into the drum, the rope is pulled tight and parts. The brake is checked by lifting a light load just off the floor, stopping and watching for drift. Pendant buttons must be labelled and spring back; a button that sticks keeps the hoist running. Run each motion slowly and listen for grinding, squealing or jerky starts, and watch the rope wind into its drum grooves.

Any defect is recorded and reported, and the crane stays out of use until it is fixed (WorkSafeBC s.14.35). Beyond the shift check, a competent person thoroughly examines the crane before first use, as often as the maker says, and at least once a year, with a signed record (Ontario Reg. 851 s.51(1)(b)); BC and Alberta call up CSA B167 for periodic inspection (WorkSafeBC s.14.2, s.14.54.1; Alberta OHS Code s.94). A failed limit switch on a 5-ton crane means no lift, even for a 4,000 lb motor.

Hooks and rope

Hooks should hang straight with no cracks, nicks or gouges and a latch that closes the throat. In BC a hook comes out at more than 15 % throat opening over new, more than 10° of twist, 10 % section loss, or as soon as it passes the maker's criteria (WorkSafeBC s.15.29). Alberta uses the maker's specifications (OHS Code s.309), and on Ontario construction projects any opening past new or any twist takes a hook out (O. Reg. 213/91 s.173(4)). Most makers set the throat limit near 5 %, so use the strictest number. A hook twisted 12° comes out of service, and you never heat or bend a hook back. Wire rope comes out for broken wires, kinks, crushing, birdcaging or corrosion, and gets a thorough inspection on the maker's schedule with a record (at least weekly on Ontario construction projects, O. Reg. 213/91 s.170).

The rope is sized for the rating. On our example 5-ton hoist with four parts of 3/8 in rope (breaking strength 15,100 lb), each part carries 10,000 ÷ 4 = 2,500 lb at rated load, a design factor of 15,100 ÷ 2,500 = 6.04. BC sets at least 5 for wire rope (WorkSafeBC s.15.6, Table 15-1); check your province. Never change the reeving or rope size from the maker's.

Lockout and load tests

Before maintenance: move the crane where it interferes least, put all controllers in the off position, open the main switch and secure it with your own personal lock and tag, try the controls to verify, hang warning signs on and below the crane, and use rail stops or a signal person if other cranes share the runway. Lockout is required in every province (WorkSafeBC Part 10; Ontario Reg. 851 ss.75–76; Alberta OHS Code Part 15), and Alberta requires procedures to prevent collisions between lifting devices (s.67). A new, altered or rebuilt crane is load tested at 125 % of rated capacity by a qualified person, with signed records (WorkSafeBC s.14.54; elsewhere the maker's test and CSA B167): 3,000 kg × 1.25 = 3,750 kg. That is not a shift check.

The formula

Test load: 125 % of rated (BC 14.54; elsewhere the maker's test)
New, altered or rebuilt crane: tested before first use
Limit switches and safety devices tested at 100 %
Done by a qualified person, with records signed
Shift check: no load on the limit, light load on the brake

In BC, a new, altered or rebuilt crane is tested with 125 % of its rated capacity before it goes into use, by a qualified person, with the results signed and kept. For a crane rated 3,000 kg that is 3,000 × 1.25 = 3,750 kg. Your shift check is different: the limit with no load, and the brake with a light load just off the floor.

Worked exam questions

These questions are from the video. Try each one before you read the solution.

WORKED EXAMPLE 1

A 5-ton hoist is reeved with 4 parts of 3/8 in wire rope (breaking strength 15,100 lb). What is the design factor at rated load, and does it meet 5 to 1?

  • A. 1.51: no
  • B. 6.04: yes
  • C. 7.55: yes
  • D. 3.02: no

Answer: B. 6.04: yes

Five tons is 10,000 lb. Over 4 parts, that's 2,500 lb a part. 15,100 over 2,500 is 6.04.

10,000 ÷ 4 = 2,500 lb a part; 15,100 ÷ 2,500 = 6.04 ≥ 5 (WorkSafeBC 15.6, Table 15-1)

6.04 is more than 5. A forgot the parts of line. C and D used the wrong number of parts.

WORKED EXAMPLE 2

Start of shift on a 5-ton bridge crane: capacity marked, hook and rope OK, horn works. On the no-load test the upper limit switch does not trip. You need to lift a 4,000 lb motor. What do you do?

  • A. Lift it, and stop well short of the top
  • B. Use the limit as your stop
  • C. Tag the crane out, record it and report it
  • D. Lift it: it's under half the rating

Answer: C. Tag the crane out, record it and report it

The load is well under the rating, but that isn't the question. A safety device failed its test. That's a defect, and a crane with a defect that affects safe operation stays out of use until it's fixed.

A failed safety device = defect: record it, report it, keep the crane out of use until it is fixed (WorkSafeBC 14.35(2)–(3))

Tag it out, record it and report it. A and D are exactly how cranes get two-blocked. And B can't work: the limit is the part that's broken.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the limit test, or riding the limit
  • Running with a sticky pendant button
  • Using a crane with no capacity marking
  • Lockout with someone else's lock
  • Load test confused with the shift check
  • Fixing a defect instead of reporting it

Test the limit every shift with no load, never ride it, and report every defect instead of working around it.
