Tandem and multi-crane lifts: load shares, tilt and the 75 % rule

Two cranes on one load, both under their charts, and one of them still tips. A tandem lift is one load on two or more cranes: long pipe spools, heat exchanger bundles, a tall vessel being stood up. Two cranes look like twice the capacity. They aren't. Each crane carries its own share, and that share moves. Millwrights rig these loads and often signal them, and the Red Seal exam asks how to work out each share and when the lift becomes critical. The crane numbers here come from one maker's product guide, for training only.

Each crane's share comes from moments

Two identical 40 ton cranes, each at 30 ft of radius where the chart gives 21,850 lb, pick a 28,000 lb skid at two points 20 ft apart. With the centre of gravity in the middle, each carries 14,000 lb. Add each crane's own hook block and rigging, 800 lb, and each is at 68 %.

Now the drawings were wrong, and the centre of gravity is 8 ft from pick A, not 10. Crane A carries the load times the distance from the centre of gravity to the other pick, over the span: 28,000 × 12 ÷ 20 = 16,800 lb. Crane B carries the other 11,200 lb. Crane A goes to 80.5 % of its chart and crane B drops to 55 %. The closer crane always carries more. Never just split the load in half.

What a tilt does

A skid hung from lugs on top has its centre of gravity below the picks; in the example, 8 ft below. If crane B's operator hoists a little faster, the skid tilts B end up and the centre of gravity swings toward the higher end. At 10° its horizontal distance from pick A grows from 10 ft to 11.24 ft over a horizontal span of 19.7 ft, so crane B carries 15,975 lb. Ten degrees of tilt moved about 2,000 lb onto one crane: from 68 % to almost 77 %. The share that moves is about the load times the height times the tangent of the tilt, over the span: 28,000 × 8 × 0.1763 ÷ 20 = 1,975 lb.

The rule of thumb: a centre of gravity below the picks moves toward the higher crane; one above the picks, as on a tall skid lifted from lugs near its base, moves toward the lower crane. Either way, keep the load level. In the failure, a 26,000 lb skid at 40 ft starts at 95.5 % of each chart, a planned critical lift. As it tilts B end up, crane B's share climbs until, at about 17°, it passes its tipping load and goes over. Neither crane was over its chart when the lift started.

The Canadian rules

In British Columbia (OHS Regulation s. 14.1), a tandem lift where any one crane carries more than 75 % of its rated capacity is a critical lift. The rule looks at each crane, not the average: 64 % and 84 % is critical. Any lift with more than two cranes is critical too, and so is a load whose centre of gravity changes during the lift. Liebherr notes that the international crane standard limits manual tandem lifts to 75 %.

Every tandem lift in BC needs a written lift plan at the worksite, covering rigging, wind limits, hoist and travel speeds, how the load is shared and where the signallers stand (s. 14.42). A critical tandem lift is directed by a qualified supervisor who isn't running either crane. In Alberta, any lift over 75 % of a crane's capacity needs a lift calculation. One signaller at a time, a documented meeting before the lift, and communication kept up the whole way. Check your province.

Planning the lift

Setting a 20 ft heat exchanger bundle with two cranes: get the centre of gravity from the drawing and work out each crane's share. Check each crane's chart at the worst radius it will reach during the lift, with its own hook block and rigging added. Keep the load level and the hook lines plumb, because a line out of plumb side-loads the boom, which the chart doesn't allow for. Match the hoist speeds and move one function at a time. One person directs, one signaller talks, and you do a radio check first. And plan for the centre of gravity moving.

The formula

A = W × b ÷ span, B = W × a ÷ span
CG below the picks → toward the higher crane; above → the lower
Shift ≈ W × h × tan α ÷ span
BC: any crane > 75 % or > 2 cranes → critical; plan every tandem lift
One qualified director; keep it level

Crane A carries the load times the distance from the centre of gravity to crane B's pick, over the span, and the other way round for B. For 28,000 lb with the CG 8 ft from A on a 20 ft span: 28,000 × 12 ÷ 20 = 16,800 lb on A, 11,200 lb on B. Then add each crane's own rigging.

Worked exam questions

These questions are from the video. Try each one before you read the solution.

WORKED EXAMPLE 1

A 28,000 lb load is picked by two cranes at points 20 ft apart. Its centre of gravity is 8 ft from crane A's pick. How much of the load does crane A carry?

  • A. 11,200 lb
  • B. 14,000 lb
  • C. 16,800 lb
  • D. 22,400 lb

Answer: C. 16,800 lb

Crane A carries the load times the distance from the centre of gravity to the other pick, over the span. That distance is 20 minus 8, 12 ft.

A = W × b ÷ span = 28,000 × (20 − 8) ÷ 20 = 16,800 lb; B = 11,200 lb

28,000 times 12 over 20. 16,800. It's C. A is crane B's share. B splits it in half. D uses the span minus nothing.

WORKED EXAMPLE 2

A 28,000 lb skid hangs from two cranes, picks 20 ft apart, CG centred and 8 ft below the picks. It tilts 10°, crane B's end up. Which crane gains load, and about how much?

  • A. Crane A, about 1,975 lb
  • B. Crane B, about 1,975 lb
  • C. Neither: the shares don't change
  • D. Crane B, about 14,000 lb

Answer: B. Crane B, about 1,975 lb

A centre of gravity below the picks swings toward the higher end. The share that moves is the load, times the height, times the tangent of the angle, over the span. 28,000 times 8 times 0.1763, over 20.

CG below the picks moves toward the higher end: shift ≈ W × h × tan α ÷ span = 28,000 × 8 × 0.1763 ÷ 20 = 1,975 lb onto B

About 1,975 lb onto crane B. It's B. A picks the wrong crane. C is only true when the centre of gravity sits right on the line through the picks. D is the whole share.

Common mistakes

  • Splitting the load in half without the CG
  • Averaging the two cranes against 75 %
  • Forgetting each crane's own hook block and rigging
  • Letting the load tilt
  • Two people directing

Find the centre of gravity before you split the load, check each crane against 75 % on its own, add each crane's own rigging, keep the load level, and have one person direct the lift.
