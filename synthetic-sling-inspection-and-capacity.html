Synthetic slings: tags, capacity, edges and inspection

A web sling rated for almost 11,000 lb in a basket can drop a 6,000 lb load if it crosses an unprotected square corner. Synthetic slings are light and kind to finished surfaces, but they cut, melt and react to chemicals in ways steel doesn't. The Red Seal exam asks about all of it. The tag values on this page are examples for training: always read your own sling's tag.

Start with the tag

No tag, or a tag you can't read, means no lift. The tag gives the maker, a code or stock number, the material and the rated load for each hitch. On our example 2 in, 2-ply polyester web sling the tag reads 6,200 lb vertical, 4,960 lb choker (80 % of vertical) and 12,400 lb basket with the legs vertical (twice the vertical). Use the figure for the hitch you're actually using: in a choker, that is 4,960 lb.

On European slings the label colour shows the material: blue for polyester, green for nylon (polyamide), brown for polypropylene.

Capacity by hitch

A choker is rated at 80 % of vertical while the angle of choke is 120° or more. Pull it tighter than that and the rating drops further: for a synthetic sling, take the reduction from the maker's chart.

A basket doubles the vertical rating with the legs straight up, then the leg angle takes some back: capacity = 2 × WLL × sin(leg angle from horizontal). At 60°, the 6,200 lb sling is good for 2 × 6,200 × 0.866 = 10,739 lb. At 45°, about 8,768 lb. The flatter the legs, the less the sling can lift.

Protect every edge

Makers call an edge sharp when its radius is no bigger than the thickness of the sling, and an edge doesn't have to look sharp to cut. In a basket at 60° the sling above is good for 10,739 lb, and the load is only 6,000 lb, but over a square steel corner with no protection the edge saws through the fibres and the sling fails. In British Columbia the regulation says that when a sling goes over a sharp edge, the edge or the sling must be protected. Use pads, sleeves or corner protectors made for the job, and set them where the sling will bear once the load is up.

What takes a synthetic sling out of service

Check the whole length before each use. Remove the sling for any of these: cuts, holes, tears or snags; red core yarns showing (red is a last warning, not the only one); burns, melting or charring; broken or worn stitching in the load-bearing splices; knots; faded, stiff or brittle webbing from sunlight; acid or caustic damage; a missing or unreadable tag. On a roundsling, a cut cover that shows the core yarns takes it out.

Material, chemicals and heat

Nylon stretches about 8 to 10 % at its rated load; polyester about 3 %. Nylon is damaged by acids; polyester resists acids but is attacked by alkalis, so near acid fumes choose polyester and check the maker's chemical chart. The BC regulation keeps synthetic web slings below 82 °C (180 °F) unless the maker allows more; many makers rate nylon and polyester to 90 °C (194 °F). Below −40 the fibres get brittle.

Roundslings are colour coded, but the codes differ. In the North American code purple is 2,600 lb vertical; on a European sling violet is 1,000 kg, about 2,205 lb, and European slings use a 7 to 1 design factor instead of 5 to 1. The colour gets you close; the tag tells you the truth. A green European roundsling rated 2,000 kg is good for 0.8 × 2,000 = 1,600 kg in a choker.

The formula

Choker × 0.8 · basket × 2 × sin angle
No tag, no lift
Protect every edge
Cuts, red yarns, burns, stitching, knots, UV: out
Nylon: no acids · polyester: no alkalis · 82 °C (B.C.)

Start from the vertical rating on the tag. A choker is 0.8 times vertical while the choke is 120° or more; a basket is 2 times vertical times the sine of the leg angle from horizontal. Then protect every edge the sling touches.

Worked exam questions

These questions are from the video. Try each one before you read the solution.

WORKED EXAMPLE 1

A web sling rated 6,200 lb vertical is used in a basket hitch with both legs at 60° from horizontal. What is the capacity?

  • A. 12,400 lb
  • B. 10,739 lb
  • C. 6,200 lb
  • D. 5,369 lb

Answer: B. 10,739 lb

A basket doubles the vertical rating, then the leg angle takes some back. 6,200, times 2, times the sine of 60, 0.866. About 10,740 lb.

Basket = 2 × WLL × sin 60° = 2 × 6,200 × 0.866 = 10,739 lb

A forgot the angle. D forgot that a basket doubles the rating.

WORKED EXAMPLE 2

A green European roundsling, tag 2,000 kg vertical, in a choker (choke over 120°) around a steel beam with a sharp flange edge. The load is 1,500 kg. What is the capacity, and is the lift ready?

  • A. 2,000 kg: ready to lift
  • B. 1,600 kg: ready to lift
  • C. 1,600 kg: protect the edge first
  • D. 4,000 kg: ready to lift

Answer: C. 1,600 kg: protect the edge first

A choker is 80 % of vertical. 2,000 times 0.8 is 1,600 kg. 1,500 is under that, about 94 %. But the sling crosses a sharp edge. Protect it first, or the edge decides how much it can lift.

Choker = 0.8 × 2,000 = 1,600 kg ≥ 1,500 kg (93.8 %) · sharp flange edge → protect it before lifting

B has the right number and the wrong answer. A and D used the wrong hitch.

Common mistakes

  • Trusting the colour, not the tag
  • Using 80 % on a choke under 120°
  • Forgetting the basket leg angle
  • No edge protection
  • Nylon near acid · polyester near alkali

Read the tag, not the colour, use the factor for your hitch, and protect every edge.
